WHY THIS CHAPTER MATTERS

Cusco does more than organise logistics. It prepares the eye.

The original route gave Cusco a clear purpose: acclimatisation, city, heritage and local atmosphere — context before the iconic stretch. That remains the smartest way to experience it: arrive, slow down, understand the former Inca capital, then move into the Sacred Valley.

LTS LENS

Altitude belongs in the itinerary. The first day does not have to prove anything; it can simply help you read the city, listen to your body and let the journey deepen.

HOW TO EXPERIENCE IT

Cusco

Cusco is the chapter of acclimatisation, heritage and context. Before the headline moment of the trip, the city rewards a more human pace — and is far more than a staging post for Machu Picchu.

Historic Centre

Plaza de Armas, Qorikancha and streets where Inca and colonial layers remain in conversation.

San Blas

Slopes, workshops and a smaller urban scale. A neighbourhood for walking slowly, not ticking boxes.

Saqsaywaman & surroundings

Where city and Andean landscape begin to merge — useful context before the Sacred Valley.

PACE & ROUTE

City first. Landscape second.

Cusco works best when arrival is not a marathon. Build context and acclimatisation before longer transfers.

An editorial route, not a fixed package.

First hours

Check in, eat lightly, take a short walk and rest if you need it. Arrival is not a fitness test.

Historic Cusco

Centre, Qorikancha, San Blas and time for markets, cafés or simply being in the city.

Surroundings

Saqsaywaman and nearby sites, with intensity adjusted to how you are feeling.

Transition

Move into the Sacred Valley and consider sleeping there rather than automatically returning to Cusco.

PLANNING ESSENTIALS

Altitude changes the order of decisions

Cusco sits close to 3,400 m. The better itinerary is not the one that squeezes more into day one; it is the one that leaves room to acclimatize.

01

When to go

For itineraries built around walking and Andean roads, drier months tend to be simpler. Mountain weather still shifts quickly, so choose the season around the experience rather than a single “perfect” month.

02

How many nights

2 nights are the comfortable minimum for city + arrival; 3 work better if you want nearby sites without racing.

03

How to arrive

Flying from Lima is the common route. Because the altitude jump is abrupt, treat the first hours as arrival, not a fitness test.

04

Book first

If Machu Picchu is part of the trip, secure its entrance circuit/route first; then align train, Sacred Valley and hotel nights around that date.

ALTITUDE, WITHOUT DRAMA — BUT WITH RESPECT

The CDC uses Cusco as a classic example of rapid arrival at high elevation. Give acclimatization time where possible, keep activity light at first, and seek medical advice if you have a pre-existing condition or significant symptoms.

DECISIONS THAT SHAPE THE TRIP

Cusco

Altitude is an itinerary decision

Cusco sits around 3,400 metres. Travellers with medical conditions or significant symptoms should seek professional advice.

Keep day one flexible

Do not lock the first hours into an inflexible agenda; individual responses vary.

Choose your base around your route

The Historic Centre is convenient for city time; the Sacred Valley calls for a different base.

Avoid the automatic day trip

With enough time, an overnight in the valley changes both pace and logistics.

FAQ

Cusco: the choices that actually change the trip

Cusco or Sacred Valley first?

Both can work. Sleeping lower in parts of the Sacred Valley can make the altitude transition gentler; Cusco first gives the journey a stronger historical opening.

Is the Sacred Valley worth an overnight?

Yes. It changes both pace and logistics and avoids treating the valley as a long commute.

Where should I stay in Cusco?

For a first visit, near the historic centre is usually easiest. If hills are an issue, factor that into the exact hotel location.

Is altitude about fitness?

Not really. Individual response varies; rate of ascent and acclimatization matter more than simply being “fit”.
