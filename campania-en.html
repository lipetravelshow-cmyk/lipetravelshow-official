Understand Naples
“Naples was not made to be admired from a distance. It must be crossed.”

The city is intense, contradictory, and deeply human. The same territory brings together Baroque art, Roman engineering, an active volcano, popular neighborhoods, and recipes that conquered the world.

The secret is not to make it orderly. The secret is to understand its layers.

MAP

Millennia of history within a short distance

Naples works as the base. Pompeii and Herculaneum lie toward Vesuvius; Pozzuoli is to the west; Caserta and Capua to the north; Capri sits in the Gulf as a full-day outing. In two days, prioritize Naples and one archaeological site. With three or four days, the trip gains depth.

A free walking tour provides context; the Cappella Sansevero and the underground city reveal how the strongest experience begins when you go beneath the surface.

Famous castle + practical notes

CASTEL NUOVO

Castel Nuovo: the fortress guarding the port

The aerial view shows why Maschio Angioino is one of Naples’ defining images: walls, towers, and the harbor meeting in the heart of the city.

Castel Nuovo / Maschio Angioino

The most strategic castle for this itinerary. It stands in Municipio, right in front of Molo Beverello. Even if you do not enter, it is worth seeing for its towers and marble triumphal arch.

How to fit it in

It works well on the same day as the historic center, Toledo, Piazza del Plebiscito, and the Lungomare. Late afternoon gives you the best visual link with the port and Capri.

Time + logistics

Allow 30 minutes to 1 hour for the exterior and the square; longer if you want to visit more calmly and combine it with Santa Lucia.

Campania is not just about ruins. It lets you walk inside the mechanisms that built the Mediterranean world.

POMPEII

Pompeii: the interrupted city

Pompeii is not an isolated field of stones. It is a full city: streets, baths, houses, frescoes, commerce, and entertainment spaces preserved by the eruption of AD 79.

AD 79

The eruption that interrupted urban life.

4–6 hours

A comfortable visit without trying to see everything.

Arrive early

Less heat and better conditions for the open areas.

HERCULANEUM

Herculaneum: the city preserved in detail

Smaller and more concentrated than Pompeii, Herculaneum offers an intimate reading of Roman life. Houses, mosaics, frescoes, and even organic elements make it a complementary experience rather than a repetitive one.

ERCOLANO

Less monumental. Closer to everyday life.

Herculaneum was struck by the same AD 79 eruption, but the way volcanic materials covered the city preserved interiors and details exceptionally well. It is a more compact visit and usually takes two to four hours.

How to get there

Take the EAV/Circumvesuviana line from Napoli Garibaldi toward Sorrento and get off at Ercolano Scavi. The walk to the entrance is short.

What to see

Roman houses, frescoes, mosaics, ancient residential areas, and a more human-scale archaeological experience. It is ideal for travelers who want detail, not only scale.

Practical tip

Bring water and sun protection. Because it is smaller, Herculaneum pairs well with a half day back in Naples or with the Lungomare area.

CAPRI

Capri: a full day on the other side of the Gulf

Capri should not be rushed between other attractions. Leaving Naples early, it works best as a full-day excursion focused on scenery, navigation, and the island’s two towns.

DAY TRIP

How to go, how much it costs, and what to do

The most balanced plan combines a boat circuit or viewpoint for the Faraglioni, time in Anacapri, and time in Capri town. The Blue Grotto depends on sea conditions and should never be treated as guaranteed.

How to go

Fast hydrofoils leave mainly from Molo Beverello in Naples. The crossing usually takes about 50 to 60 minutes, depending on the vessel and daily operation.

Price range

As a reference, one-way tickets are often around €24–€30, and round trip about €48–€60 per person. Prices vary by season, departure time, and operator.

What to do

Boat tour to see the Faraglioni, Capri town with the Piazzetta and Gardens of Augustus, and a trip up to Anacapri for Monte Solaro or Villa San Michele.

Tip

Tip: in summer, buy your ferry early and do not leave your return for the last boat. Sea and wind can affect operations.

EXTENSIONS

Other impossible masterpieces

Not every experience fits into the same time frame. Use this curation to shape the trip around your interests.

Royal palace

Reggia di Caserta

Built to rival Versailles, it astonishes with its monumental scale, Baroque interiors, and kilometers of gardens, fountains, and carefully framed perspectives. Allow a full day if architecture, landscape design, and Bourbon history are central to your trip.

Phlegraean Fields

Pozzuoli

The Flavian Amphitheater and the Macellum reveal a less obvious Campania, shaped by Roman engineering and volcanic ground movement. Pair the ruins with the waterfront to see archaeology, sea, and geology sharing the same landscape.

Ancient Rome

Capua

The Campanian Amphitheater, one of the largest in the Roman Empire, preserves the memory of the gladiator school associated with Spartacus. It is a powerful visit for understanding the political, military, and theatrical scale of ancient Campania.
