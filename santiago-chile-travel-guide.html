How I would do it

Santiago works better as a smart base than a checklist

Santiago works best when the trip has a clear rhythm: an efficient base, time for walks and viewpoints, and separate days for the extensions that truly matter to you.

Three nights is the sweet spot

Two nights cover the essentials; three usually feels more elegant, and four makes sense when Santiago carries real weight in the itinerary.

Alternate city and views

Center, neighborhoods, parks and higher viewpoints work better interspersed than packed into exhausting blocks.

Choose the neighborhood before the hotel

Where you stay changes the trip more than small differences between similarly priced properties.

Think about what comes next

Santiago can be the main destination or a gateway to the Andes, snow, wine regions, Atacama and the rest of Chile.

LIPE'S TAKE

Santiago works better when the itinerary has room to breathe

I would use Santiago as an intentional base: choose the neighborhood before the hotel, alternate city life with viewpoints, and keep snow, wine or the Pacific for separate days. The city does not need to become a race through attractions to feel rewarding.
